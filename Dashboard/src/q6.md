# Q6: Did on-time performance keep pace with traffic?

**Research question:** Did on-time performance keep pace with traffic, and which airports and carriers are most delay-prone, when, and why?

The question has two parts, so the page has two views:

- **View 1** compares two changes at each busy airport between 2018 and 2025: traffic and the on-time arrival rate. Did performance keep pace?
- **View 2** ranks airports or airlines by delay minutes per flight in 2025, split by cause, with each one's worst month. Which, why and when?

<div class="note" label="Definitions">

- **Busy airports:** the 72 large and medium hubs of 2025 (0.25% or more of national passengers, the Q5 groups).
- **Traffic:** performed departures from the airport (BTS T-100, Class F).
- **On time:** arrived less than 15 minutes after schedule. Only flights that were not cancelled or diverted and have a recorded arrival delay count.
- **Arrivals belong to the destination airport,** where the flight landed.
- **On-time source:** BTS Reporting Carrier On-Time Performance. It covers only the larger airlines (0.5% or more of domestic scheduled passenger revenue), so rates describe those airlines' flights, not every flight.
- **Why 2018:** the reporting threshold changed in 2018. The set of reporting airlines can still change between years, so each airport's data coverage is shown.

</div>

## What does this visualization show?

- **On-time performance did not keep pace.** The on-time rate fell at 68 of the 71 busy airports (median -3.2 points).
- **Traffic growth does not explain it.** It fell at airports that grew, like Denver, and at airports that flew fewer flights, like Detroit. The only three that improved, Newark, San Francisco and Houston Hobby, all flew fewer flights.
- **Most delay-prone in 2025:** Newark (24.7 delay minutes per flight), then Washington National, Dallas/Fort Worth, Philadelphia and Chicago O'Hare. New York and Washington area airports fill much of the top ten.
- **Why:** at 59 of the 72 busy airports, the biggest cause is a late incoming aircraft, a delay carried over from an earlier flight. At Newark it is the air traffic system.
- **When:** July is the worst month at most airports (51 of 72), December at 12 more.
- **Airlines:** PSA Airlines (flying as American Eagle) and Frontier had the most delay minutes per flight; Hawaiian, Alaska and Southwest the fewest.

These are associations, not causes: View 1 shows that on-time rates and traffic moved together only weakly, not why on-time rates fell. Delay causes are what airlines report to BTS.

## How the data was prepared

1. `pipeline/download_ontime.py` downloads the 132 monthly BTS On-Time files (2015-2025).
2. `pipeline/prep_ontime.py` reads them and aggregates to destination airport x airline x month: flights, cancellations, valid arrivals, on-time arrivals and summed minutes for each delay cause. Rates are always computed later as ratios of these sums, so they are weighted by flights. The notebook's Q6 cells use the same definitions; the Milestone 2 version grouped arrivals by the origin airport.
3. `pipeline/prep_q6.py` joins T-100 departures and the Q5 groups, applies the 50% coverage rule, and writes the three tables used on this page.
